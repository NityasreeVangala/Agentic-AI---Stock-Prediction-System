# **Day 11 – Learning Fundamental Metrics (Beginner Friendly)**

### **Goal:**

Understand **key financial metrics** that investors use to evaluate a stock:

1. **PE Ratio (Price-to-Earnings)**
2. **EPS (Earnings Per Share)**
3. **ROE (Return on Equity)**
4. **Debt / Leverage**

These metrics tell you **how profitable, efficient, and risky a company is**.

---

## **1️⃣ PE Ratio (Price-to-Earnings)**

### **What it is:**

* Shows **how much investors are willing to pay for $1 of earnings**.
* Compares **stock price** to **company earnings**.
* Formula:

[
\text{PE Ratio} = \frac{\text{Price per Share}}{\text{Earnings per Share (EPS)}}
]

---

### **Example:**

* Stock price = ₹200
* EPS = ₹10

[
\text{PE Ratio} = \frac{200}{10} = 20
]

✅ Interpretation:

* Investors pay **20 times the earnings** for this stock.
* High PE → stock might be **expensive** (or growth expected)
* Low PE → stock might be **cheap** (or company struggling)

---

### **Python Example:**

```python
price = 200
eps = 10
pe_ratio = price / eps
print("PE Ratio:", pe_ratio)  # Output: 20
```

---

## **2️⃣ EPS (Earnings Per Share)**

### **What it is:**

* Measures **profitability per share**.
* Formula:

[
\text{EPS} = \frac{\text{Net Income} - \text{Dividends on Preferred Stock}}{\text{Number of Outstanding Shares}}
]

---

### **Example:**

* Net income = ₹50,00,000
* Outstanding shares = 5,00,000

[
\text{EPS} = \frac{50,00,000}{5,00,000} = 10
]

✅ Interpretation:

* Each share earned ₹10 profit in the last year.
* Higher EPS → company is more **profitable**.

---

### **Python Example:**

```python
net_income = 5000000
outstanding_shares = 500000
eps = net_income / outstanding_shares
print("EPS:", eps)  # Output: 10
```

---

## **3️⃣ ROE (Return on Equity)**

### **What it is:**

* Measures **how efficiently a company uses shareholders’ money** to generate profit.
* Formula:

[
\text{ROE} = \frac{\text{Net Income}}{\text{Shareholder's Equity}} \times 100
]

---

### **Example:**

* Net income = ₹50,00,000
* Shareholder equity = ₹2,50,00,000

[
\text{ROE} = \frac{50,00,000}{2,50,00,000} \times 100 = 20%
]

✅ Interpretation:

* Company generates **20% profit for every ₹1 of equity**.
* Higher ROE → company is using money **efficiently**.

---

### **Python Example:**

```python
net_income = 5000000
equity = 25000000
roe = (net_income / equity) * 100
print("ROE:", roe, "%")  # Output: 20%
```

---

## **4️⃣ Debt / Leverage**

### **What it is:**

* Shows how **much debt a company has compared to its equity**.
* Formula (Debt-to-Equity Ratio):

[
\text{Debt-to-Equity} = \frac{\text{Total Debt}}{\text{Shareholder's Equity}}
]

---

### **Example:**

* Total debt = ₹1,00,00,000
* Equity = ₹2,50,00,000

[
\text{Debt-to-Equity} = \frac{1,00,00,000}{2,50,00,000} = 0.4
]

✅ Interpretation:

* For every ₹1 of equity, company has ₹0.4 of debt.
* Lower ratio → safer, less risky.
* Higher ratio → more leveraged → higher risk.

---

### **Python Example:**

```python
total_debt = 10000000
equity = 25000000
de_ratio = total_debt / equity
print("Debt-to-Equity:", de_ratio)  # Output: 0.4
```

## **Summary of Day 11 Metrics**

| Metric          | Formula             | What it tells you                     |
| --------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **PE**          | Price ÷ EPS         | How expensive a stock is              |
| **EPS**         | Net Income ÷ Shares | Profit per share                      |
| **ROE**         | Net Income ÷ Equity | Efficiency of using shareholder money |
| **Debt/Equity** | Total Debt ÷ Equity | Financial risk / leverage             |
