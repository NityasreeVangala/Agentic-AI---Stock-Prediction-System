# 🔹 PART 1 (0:00–0:40)

## What Are NSE and BSE — Really?

### ❌ Beginner misunderstanding

> “NSE and BSE are companies that tell stock prices.”

### ✅ Reality

NSE and BSE are:

> **Exchanges** — marketplaces where buyers and sellers meet.

They do **not** decide prices.
They only **record trades**.

---

### Why India has two exchanges

* **BSE** → older, more stocks
* **NSE** → higher liquidity, faster trading

Most analytics use **NSE data** for indicators
But BSE is useful for **cross-checking**

---

### Important mental note

> Same company ≠ same price always (NSE vs BSE)

Differences happen due to:

* Liquidity
* Timing
* Volume

---

# 🔹 PART 2 (0:40–1:30)

## OHLCV — Explained Like You’ve Never Seen It

Every row in stock data represents **one trading day**.

Let’s take an example row:

```
Date        Open    High    Low     Close   Volume
2024-01-02  2450    2480    2430    2475    3,200,000
```

---

## 🧠 What each column REALLY means

### 🔸 Open

> Price of **first trade** of the day
> Not yesterday’s close

---

### 🔸 High

> Highest price **anyone was willing to pay** that day

---

### 🔸 Low

> Lowest price **anyone accepted to sell** that day

---

### 🔸 Close ⭐ (MOST IMPORTANT)

> Price of **last trade of the day**

📌 Why close price matters:

* Institutions evaluate on close
* Indicators are calculated on close
* You use **close price for prediction**

---

### 🔸 Volume

> Number of shares traded that day

High volume = strong conviction
Low volume = weak movement

---

### Key Insight #1

> Price movement without volume is suspicious.

---

# 🔹 PART 3 (1:30–2:15)

## Trading Days, Holidays & Missing Dates

### ❌ Beginner mistake

> “Why are dates missing? Is my data broken?”

### ✅ Reality

Markets:

* Don’t trade on weekends
* Don’t trade on holidays
* Stop during extreme events

---

### Example

If Friday is 5 Jan
Next row might be 8 Jan (Monday)

That is **normal**

---

### Why this matters for ML

Models assume:

> Equal time steps

But stock data:

> Has irregular gaps

---

### Your rule

* **Never forward-fill prices blindly**
* Use business-day aware logic

---

### Key Insight #2

> Missing dates ≠ missing data

---

# 🔹 PART 4 (2:15–3:00)

## Corporate Actions (THIS IS WHERE MOST PEOPLE FAIL)

Corporate actions **change price without changing value**.

---

## 🔥 Stock Split (Very Important)

### Example

Before split:

* Price = ₹1000
* Shares = 1

2:1 Split:

* Price becomes ₹500
* Shares become 2

Your money stays same.

---

### What happens to data?

If you don’t adjust:

* Price suddenly drops 50%
* Model thinks company crashed

---

### Solution

Use **adjusted prices**

---

## 🔥 Dividends

When company pays dividend:

* Price drops on ex-dividend date
* Not because company is weak

---

### Example

* Price: ₹100
* Dividend: ₹5
* New price ≈ ₹95

---

### Beginner trap

Model thinks:

> “Big drop = bearish signal”

But it’s not.

---

### Key Insight #3

> Corporate actions create **fake volatility**.
